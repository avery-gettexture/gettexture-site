# Texture — Brand Voice and Identity
## Foundational Document v1.0

This document captures the brand planning session conducted in March 2026. The language here is precise and was arrived at deliberately through a structured conversation between the founder and Claude. Maintain fidelity to this language throughout all product decisions.

---

## What Texture Is

Texture is an astrology app for people who are done with the irony but not ready for the deep end. It treats astrology as what it actually is — thousands of years of humans mapping astronomical events to lived experience — and presents that data seriously without asking anyone to believe anything. It is intellectually honest about what astrology is and isn't, and that honesty is exactly what makes it feel safe to engage with genuinely.

---

## Who It's For

Someone who learns the way water moves — not toward a destination, but finding its way through everything. Curious without agenda. They don't perform their interests and they don't hide them. They're slightly tired of how astrology is culturally packaged but they're still here because something about it keeps being interesting. They want a tool that respects that.

More specifically:

- They value learning and curiosity. They see learning new things as raising the floor of what they know so they can continue exploring — not following some determined path of "knowable things." Learnings are to be embraced and embodied.
- They believe everything is connected — learning about one thing illuminates all other things.
- They probably have another astrology app already — likely Co-Star. They are unlikely to have a more serious astrology app unless they're switching for aesthetic reasons.
- They recognize that astrology apps are kind of cringey right now. Astrology exists in a half-irony space culturally — so many people half embrace it but treat it with enough irony to have plausible deniability. This person is right on the other side of that — ready to learn more, but not so committed they'll download Time Passages or listen to podcasts.
- They probably pay attention to informative astrology influencers on social media.
- They find existing astrology apps either too oracle/witchy, too self-help, or too gimmicky. None of them let themselves take it seriously as an intellectual experience. But intellectual experiences are not mutually exclusive with joyful, expressive, or fun experiences. This person finds joy through learning.
- They don't identify with the nerd culture that appreciates being "othered" by society. They find genuine joy, curiosity, and intrigue in deep niche experiences — but that's a tool for expression and navigating the world, not a performed personality trait.
- They don't complain. They don't blame their environment. They learn from it. They walk away from judgment and misery for the sake of it. They engage in interesting conversations and genuinely connect with themselves and with people. They see everything as a reflective experience.
- They find clever fun engaging. They value quiet confidence over performed seriousness.

## What Texture Believes

- Art is everywhere.
- Movement is inevitable.
- Everything is in passing.
- Time is impossible to waste.
- The present matters.
- Self-knowledge is not self-improvement. Paying attention to something — really paying attention — is its own reward. The present moment has texture, and most tools flatten it.

---

## What Texture Refuses To Do

- Personify planets.
- Tell people what to do.
- Make them feel like they've joined something.
- Perform mysticism.
- Reward cringe.
- Take itself so seriously it becomes inaccessible.
- Position basic entry into astrology as buying into a belief system.

---

## The Brand Tone

Quietly confident. Intellectually warm. Never explains itself. Doesn't need to convince you of anything — just puts something real in front of you and trusts you with it. Like a very good museum exhibit, or a conversation with someone who knows a lot but doesn't need you to know that they know a lot.

**The brand does not:**
- Argue
- Perform
- Over-explain
- Use hype language
- Use therapy mimicry
- Use spiritual bypass tone
- Make overly deterministic claims

**The brand does:**
- State things
- Trust the user
- Deliver precision without fanfare
- Let silence do work

## The Competitive Landscape — What Texture Stands Against

**Co-Star:** Too oracular. Positions astrology as a belief system. Tone is cold and declarative in a way that performs mysticism.

**The Pattern:** Too oracular. Feels like being told who you are rather than invited to observe.

**CHANI:** Paywalls too many meaningful things. Does what so many astrology apps do — personification of planets. Planets treated almost like gods. Creates an audience but loses the anthropological dimension.

**Time Passages:** Great technical depth but too dated. Requires a commitment to developing chart literacy that presents a steep learning curve. Too prescriptive in what things mean.

**The category as a whole:** Instead of engaging in an interesting anthropological experience — thousands of years of humans mapping events to astronomical planet positions — planets are treated almost like gods. Too many apps position basic entry into astrology as buying into a belief system instead of presenting facts in front of people so they can do with it what they will.

**The Astrology Podcast** is the closest reference point for the right register — it takes itself seriously but doesn't pedestal itself. Texture is like that, but way less intense. The light version, suitable for people who know what astrology is, are interested in learning more and engaging with a little more attention, but are still relative beginners.

## Visual Identity — What Texture Looks Like

### The Splash Screen (the true brand statement)

The splash screen was the moment the visual identity became clear. It features a full-screen red field that behaves like a physical surface — three planetary bodies orbit the center at different speeds and radii, and each one generates expanding wave interference across the entire field as it moves, like stones dropped in water. Where the wave systems from different planets intersect, they create authentic constructive and destructive interference patterns — brighter crests where waves reinforce, darker troughs where they cancel. The word TEXTURE sits at the center as a solid darker-red shape, slightly translucent, feeling like it's carved into or emerging from the surface rather than sitting on top of it.

The overall effect is of a living material — the red field has physical properties, the planets are forces acting on it, and the name exists as a feature of the surface itself. This is the most honest visual metaphor for what astrology actually is.

### The Visual Tension

There is a specific tension in the visual identity: whenever the app name TEXTURE is visualized, it calls for something bolder than the typical astrology aesthetic. The interference wave idea is not decorative — it is the most honest visual metaphor for what astrology actually is. The monochrome boldness — all shades of one color, the name as a feature of the surface not sitting on top — is a brand that knows itself.

**Red was chosen deliberately.** Red doesn't really exist in space (outside of Mars), but it feels like a very human color. The dichotomy of astrology's current soft, atmospheric cultural positioning versus the boldness of the red interference image is an intentional and engaging counterpoint.

### Reference Points

These are the things that already exist in the world that feel like Texture:

**Dalí:** Technical precision in service of impossible content. The skill makes the strangeness more unsettling, not less. Applied to Texture — visual execution should be so clean and considered that the strangeness of the content lands harder, not softer.

**Sculptures:** The experience requires your participation. You have to move around it. Applied to Texture — the app shouldn't give you everything at once. Depth should be revealed through engagement. Things should feel like they have a back side.

**Epic nature:** The kind of beauty that feels constructed, almost too intentional to be accidental. Not soft beauty — structural beauty. Canyons, not meadows. Waves mid-ocean, not a lake. Applied to Texture — the visual language should have the quality of feeling inevitable and slightly impossible at the same time.

**Wes Anderson:** Completely controlled. Every frame is a decision. Nothing is accidental. The world is its own internally consistent reality — you step into it, it doesn't meet you halfway. But it's warm. It's never cold. Applied to Texture — total visual consistency, a world that has rules you can feel even if you can't name them, and genuine warmth underneath the precision.

**Black holes:** Staring into the abyss in a way that studying astrology can feel. It is a faith/spiritual experience for people culturally and it can feel kind of existential when an astrology thing really hits home. Texture knows this and prepares the user for it — not by performing mysticism but by introducing a little strangeness so people aren't caught off guard when the content starts to feel strange.

### The Visual System — Current State

The app is currently regrounding into a new visual system. Key values:

**Cream** (`#FDF5ED`) is the grounding system color — the baseline surface. All onboarding and navigation lives on cream.

**Red** (`rgba(185,18,18,0.75)` / `rgba(185,18,18,0.55)`) is the brand accent. Used for the TEXTURE wordmark, active states, payoff lines, CTAs that need presence.

**Indigo** (`#0e0c1a`) is used for immersive moments — chart loading, the belt zone on the home screen. Dark and sky tones are used intentionally, not as the default.

**Typography:**
- Anton — brand display, wordmark, moments of presence
- Questrial — content, body text, values in forms
- Geist Mono — labels, metadata, navigation, fine print

**Authority Ratio:** 70% clarity, 30% poetic elevation. Never invert.

**"Your chart is a map of one specific moment in the sky. Thousands of years of observation went into understanding what that moment means. This is your texture."**

This is the first substantive thing the app says to a new user. It is the brand's complete statement about itself — no tagline needed.
